      How BLE capture works · limitations, privacy, and what the packet names mean
      Three primary channels

      BLE devices may advertise on channels 37, 38, and 39. In “All 3” mode, AnalyzerPro assigns one radio to each so they are monitored simultaneously. Pinning a channel puts all radios on that primary channel and can miss connection requests sent on the other two.

      Native connection following

      A CONNECT_IND supplies the access address, timing, channel map, and hop information. AnalyzerPro firmware uses it to stay synchronized and receive that connection as it moves across data channels 0–36; the driver labels those packets and their direction.

      PHY selection

      LE 1M is the best beginner default and is used for legacy primary advertising. Select LE 2M or LE Coded S=8/S=2 only when the device and test scenario use that PHY. A connection can later request a PHY change with LL_PHY control packets.

      Encryption

      Encrypted payloads remain protected unless you already know the 128-bit LTK and the required encryption setup is captured. Supplying an LTK is passive and local; it does not discover keys or recover LE Secure Connections keys from a passkey.

      Packet names and direction

      ADV and CONNECT names describe discovery. LL_DATA carries higher-layer traffic, while LL_* control names explain connection changes such as channel maps, encryption, length, and PHY. Central → peripheral and the reverse show who sent each followed packet.

      RSSI is an estimate

      A rising (less negative) RSSI means a stronger received signal, but bodies, walls, antenna direction, transmit power, and small radio-to-radio calibration differences affect it. Compare each channel's trend; never treat it as metres.

      Passive capture only

      The analyzer observes traffic already on the air; it does not connect to or query a device. Names and services appear only when broadcast or visible in captured traffic, so this is not a complete GATT inventory.

      Privacy addresses

      Phones and wearables can rotate random BLE addresses. A device may therefore reappear as a new row. Capture only devices you own or are authorized to test, and avoid retaining personal payload data.
